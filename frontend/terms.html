We do not host it, store it, transcode it, re-encode it or re-transmit it, and we do not remove or replace the advertising those providers insert. 3. Households, profiles and seats

A subscription covers one household. Within it you can create viewing profiles, invite household members up to your seat limit, and set what each profile is allowed to watch.

  • Profiles are viewing modes, not accounts. Household members you invite get their own login and count against your seats.
  • Kids profiles restrict what can be played, using the rating information the content providers supply. Content with no rating is treated as adult and withheld from kids profiles by default.
  • A parental PIN keeps a profile switch behind a prompt. It is a household convenience, not a security control, and it will not stop a determined or technically capable child.
  • Rating information comes from third parties and can be missing, wrong or inconsistent between the systems it comes from. You remain responsible for supervising what your household watches. We do not warrant that any filter is complete or accurate.

Hosting a watch party spends credits. One credit is ten minutes of hosting time, measured on the clock and unaffected by how many people are watching. Subscribers receive a monthly allowance which rolls over up to a ceiling; anything above that ceiling is lost rather than banked. You can buy a top-up of 24 credits for 1 US dollar as a one-off payment.

  • Credits are a feature of the service, not currency, not stored value and not property. They have no cash value, cannot be transferred, sold or exchanged, and are not redeemable for money.
  • Unspent credits, including purchased ones, are forfeited when your account is closed or deleted.
  • Joining a party costs nothing. Only the host spends credits.
  • Hosting requires an active subscription.
